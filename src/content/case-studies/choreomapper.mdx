This project addressed the lack of tools available for dance practitioners
to adequately document and archive their complex and often non-linear
careers. Through this project, I co-designed an interactive spiral
timeline, providing a novel proposal for a personal data visualization
model for the dance & HCI field.

> How can a co-designed interactive visualization tool effectively
> capture, archive and communicate the complex, multi-faceted and
> non-linear career paths of female dance practitioners?
>
> — Thesis' Research Question

### Project Context

The inception of this project took place in Basque Country, specifically
Biarritz, France. Bertha Martinez, dancer and researcher, noticed and
addressed a lack of communication between the French and the Spanish
communities of traditional Basque dancers. Though practicing the same
dance and being a part of similar communities, there was no active sharing
of their practice.

She reached out to my supervisor and her PhD student to start
collaborating on a project to bridge gaps and bring visibility to female
dance practitioners.

### Personal Motivation

I myself am a dancer who practices styles from club dances (e.g., shuffle
dance, house dance) and my experience with documenting my dance practice
and my reflections throughout my own dance trajectory influenced my
motivation and positioning as the primary researcher.

## Interviews

### Semi-Structured Interviews

I conducted two semi-structured interviews with dance practitioners from
urban/club dance styles: dancehall and shuffle dance.

As the interviewer, I used an interview guide to hear and learn about
their journey as a dancer — from their first interests in dance to where
they were at the time of interviewing — while probing for objective and
subjective data.

- **Objective:** dance & life-related events (year & duration, location,
  dance company/institution, people involved, funding, role, object of
  interest, …)
- **Subjective:** narrative of career path, artistic essence, intentions,
  inspirations, desires

## Co-Design Workshop 1: Ideating the Cartography

### Ideation Workshop with an Internal Team

I led a workshop with our internal team (me, 1 HCI PhD student, 1 HCI
Postdoc, my supervisor, and an information visualization researcher) to
**ideate a visualization for a shared design space across multiple dance
trajectories**, focusing on how to:

- Distinguish the different themes
- Present and attach different media

<ImagePlaceholder label="Workshop 1 artifacts" count={4} />

### Low Fidelity Prototype

I decided to continue with a cartographic aesthetic for the visualization,
using concentric circles that mimic topographic cartographies.

In this metaphor, concentric circles represented individual events in a
dancer's career timeline, and the themes would be distinguished visually
(e.g., a different color for each theme). Dancers with events associated
with similar dance styles would be clustered closer together.

At this point in the thesis project, I scoped down the focus of my work to
an individual dance practitioner's career trajectory — both its visual and
interaction design. To quickly contextualize this next goal for an
ideation workshop focused on that, I quickly prototyped a low-fidelity
concept of the larger cartographic design, using paper, pen and a video
editing software, CapCut.

### Ideation Workshop with a Larger Group of Researchers

To ideate on the visualization for an individual dance practitioner, I led
another workshop with the Ex)Situ research group as a whole, which
involved 17 people (those from the previous workshop, and more HCI
researchers and their PhD students).

The workshop began with a brief introduction to the project and the
proposed visual metaphor of a topographically inspired cartography of
dancers, represented by a visualization of their career trajectories. I
then prompted participants to create artifacts with the goal of
encapsulating the events of a dancer's timeline, all while considering:

- The different themes
- Various media attached to events (e.g., notes, videos)
- Connections events have to other dancers

## First 2 Figma Prototype Iterations

### Building the Spiral

The second workshop produced a variety of visual metaphors to represent a
dancer's trajectory. The one that I chose due to its alignment with the
nonlinearity of the interviewees' experiences was a spiral-shaped
timeline.

Below is the first iteration of a high-fidelity prototype made with Figma,
supporting the following experiences:

- Exploration of events
- Highlighting a specific time period to display
- Toggling to a linear view

### Putting the Spiral in Context

To further extend the previous prototype, I created another prototype
which highlights the context in which the dancer's trajectory is placed.

I did so by calling back to the original metaphor of the shared
cartographic space to bring visibility to the larger context of the
dancers' narratives: the communities in which they take space in through
their practice. Other visual cues such as colors and shapes of the
events' representations are meant to reflect themes in their narratives
and subjective data (e.g., a spiky red shape may represent an injury).

## Third Figma Prototype Iteration

### Another Iteration Before Testing

Before presenting the prototype to female dancers to evaluate, I iterated
on the design of the individual's timeline to include a mockup of what
they could do when adding an event to their timeline:

- Adding information (title, location, date, notes)
- Customizing the appearance of the event on the timeline (shape & color)
- Adding media to the event

The timeline prototype was populated with events from my own dance
career's trajectory.

## User Testing, Analysis & Future Work

### User Test with a Notion Page

To evaluate the prototype's design and its alignment on female dancers'
perception of their career trajectories, I created a hybrid probe using a
Notion page with questionnaires and the interactive prototype embedded
into the page.

What I probed for:

- First impressions of the visualization
- What parts of their dance journey they'd want to document
- Thoughts on having all dance documentation in one place
- Reactions to the timeline's design, aesthetics, symbolism
- Suggestions on improving the tool
- Whether or not they'd use it in their own archival practice

### Insights from the Hybrid Probe

Using thematic analysis, I generated 3 themes that guide future proposals
for future work for personal data visualization for dance practitioners:

- Desire for connection/community features
- Customizability of the visualization and the space it exists in
- Flexibility of expression (i.e., mobile versus desktop, and the amount
  of effort required to create their timeline)

### Potential Future Work

- Taking the work back to Biarritz, France
- Community map view
- Templates & sandbox timeline creation
- Re-exploration into data physicalizations
- More participatory methods with dancers on further design iterations
- A mobile-first design for the web-app (e.g., a PWA app)
